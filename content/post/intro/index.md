# My name is Alana!

I am an IT student based in the mountain west. I started my homelab a year ago as a way to explore technology and improve my skills. I would consider myself a beginner in tech so nothing in my blog posts should be considered a tutorial or even advice at all. I will simply fumble around with my server and maybe my documentation might help a beginner having a similar issue; or provide entertainment for those more technically inclined. Regardless, I am going to set up some stuff I find cool or practical and have fun while doing so!

---

## How/when did I get into tech?

I have had my nose in a computer my whole life however I never delved into just how they work. Don't get me wrong of course I was modding minecraft and poking holes in my network so me and my friends could play on my server. But it was never anything more than that. 

However in 9th grade my PC totally died and wouldn't turn on anymore. After some troubleshooting and frantic googling I figured out my power supply was shot. So being a teenager short on cash I figured I could just order a new part and install it simple as that. 

Unfortunately it was not as simple as I thought. I had never even **SEEN** the inside of a computer let alone taken one apart. So I opened my case and just stared at the inside for a few minutes totally confused. Why did there have to be so many wires?! So I took as many pictures as I could and then started gutting it. I had zero concept of what each wire did or why it went where it did. After getting my dead PSU removed I went to install the new one and I was totally freaked out. It took me maybe 2 hours of googling, reviewing my pictures, and a little bit of crying thinking I was going to brick my expensive gaming PC. However, I finally finished and the satisfaction of clicking that power button and it finally humming to life was overwhelming. I was hooked.

After that I wanted to start messing with some of the older junk PCs I had lying around. I wanted to delve deeper and figure out what all of these random hunks of plastic actually were. 

![Dissecting my old PC...](pctakeapart.jpg)

At this time some of my online friends were studying for their Nocti exam. I ended up taking practice tests with them over discord and getting super enthralled with the strange jargon of it all. But I was enrolled in an art school, focusing on visual arts. We didn't have a single computer class, let alone one as advanced as what they were taking in tech school. So my interest was stamped out a bit other than the few times we would continue taking practice tests or talking about specs for their new PC builds.

Then when I finally finished my Junior year I moved to a different state. This meant enrolling in a totally new school for my Senior year, which was incredibly devastating as a mega introvert. There was one saving grace however, and that was my new school offering classes in both scripting and computer hardware/operating systems. I was elated I prioritized taking these classes over everything else.

I showed up the first day and was practicalling bubbling over with excitement. Though as I stepped in and sat down at my seat I felt a bit out of place. I was the only girl in the entire class of 30 teens. This was nothing super new to me as a gamer all of the games and online spaces I frequented were scattered with the very rare girl. But this feeling in person was daunting, some of them were already getting into heated discussions about tech and using words I couldn't recognize. I immediately made up my mind that I would shut up completely and not speak a work unless I absolutely had to; after all my worst nightmare would be to say something stupid and embarass myself. 

So a semester passes and I am absolutely enthralled with this class. Learning the basics of how computers function was fascinating. I went home and purchased a few textbooks for the CompTIA A+. During this time I started having attendance issues, the other classes I was taking were interesting; however I was struggling to make friends and was having intense anxiety feeling alone in this new school. So unfortunately I started skipping. I would spend my first two periods at a nearby coffee shop reading and taking notes on my CompTIA study guides instead of attending class. Then I would go back to school and only attend my art class and my scripting/computer hardware class. This was great for learning tech, yet got me in hot water with the school as I was skipping so many classes. 

I managed to skirt by with decent enough attendance (after all my grades were still good), and at the end of the year we took the TestOut finals for OS and hardware. When I finished that test I was shaking so hard and could feel my heartbeat through my chest. It felt like more than a test. It felt like an overall judgement of my efforts and my intelligence as a whole. But of course I was being incredibly dramatic, after all it was a beginner test. So I passed and felt like I was floating as I went home. I felt like I finally had a distinct proof of my progress; from crying on my bedroom floor from a simple PSU replacement, to now knowing about all these parts I was replacing and knowing what they did. 

So after I graduated I immediately applied at my local community college to major in Information Technology. This was one of the best decisions of my life as it gave my interest in tech a jumping off point for me to soar. I devoured anything and everything in these classes. Yes, I was the annoying student who would raise my hand every two seconds to ask a question.

I got hired for my first job in IT in 2024 and then graduated with my Associate in 2025. Now I am searching for more opportunities to develop my skills and prove myself in my career. Hence, why I am cultivating my homelab.
